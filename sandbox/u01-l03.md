# Universe Sandbox: What causes day and night?

**Status:** Draft. **Every step is [unverified].** Steps were written from the community wiki (which warns that the interface changes often) and the general layout described in `PLAN.md` §6. We confirm or fix each one on Belu's screen before removing a tag.
**Goal:** Watch Earth spin in sunlight, see that the Sun always lights one half while Earth turns underneath, then slow the spin and see the day get longer.
**Start from:** The Universe Sandbox Home menu, after the Unit 0 orientation.
**Time:** about 10 minutes.

## Before you start
- Finish the Unit 0 orientation first, so the camera, pause, and speed controls are familiar.
- At the end, close the simulation without saving, so the changed Earth isn't kept.

## Steps

1. **Do:** Open the Home menu (three horizontal lines, top left), then choose **Open**. **You should see:** a list of the simulations that come with the app. [unverified]
2. **Do:** Pick a simulation that contains Earth and the Sun. Look for one named something like "Earth, Moon & Sun" or "Earth & Moon". If there is none, pick "Solar System". **You should see:** the simulation loads with Earth visible. [unverified: exact simulation names]
3. **Do:** Press **Space** (or click the Play/Pause button on the bottom bar) to pause. **You should see:** everything stops moving, and the Play/Pause button changes. [unverified]
4. **Do:** Find Earth and put the camera on it: open the Sim menu's **Search**, then click **Earth**. **You should see:** the camera centers on Earth. [unverified]
5. **Do:** Zoom in until Earth fills about half the screen (mouse scroll wheel). Turn the camera (drag with the mouse) until you can see both the bright side and the dark side, with the day–night line down the middle. **You should see:** one half lit, one half dark, and a soft line between them. That line is the **terminator**. [unverified: camera controls]
6. **Do:** Click the time display on the bottom bar to open the Time menu. Set **Target Simulation Speed** to about **1 hour per second**, or use **Slower/Faster** until you get close. Then press **Space** to play. **You should see:** Earth turns once in about 24 seconds. Continents slide from the dark side into the light, across, and back into the dark. [unverified]
7. **Do:** Watch one landmark, such as the Gulf of Mexico or Texas, go all the way around once. Say out loud when it has sunrise, noon, sunset, and midnight. **You should see:** the lit half stays facing the Sun the whole time, while the land moves through it. [unverified: whether city lights or a clear surface texture make Texas easy to spot]
8. **Do:** Pause (**Space**). Right-click Earth to open its properties panel. **You should see:** a panel with a preview of Earth at the top and several tabs below it. [unverified]
9. **Do:** Look through the tabs for Earth's **rotation period** (it may be called "Rotation Period", "Day Length", or "Rotation"). Note the value: it should be about 23.9 hours (one turn against the stars). Change it to **48 hours**. [unverified: tab and field names]
10. **Do:** Press **Space** to play again at the same speed. **You should see:** Earth now takes about twice as long to turn, so each daytime and each night lasts about twice as long. [unverified]
11. **Do:** Open the Time menu and choose **Restart**, or close the simulation without saving. **You should see:** Earth back to its normal rotation. [unverified]

## What to look for
- The Sun's light always falls on the side of Earth that faces the Sun. The bright half doesn't move around Earth. Earth turns under it.
- About one full turn of Earth = one day and one night for every place on it.
- A slower spin = a longer day.

## What it means
Day and night happen because Earth spins in the Sun's light. Wherever you are, the spin carries you into the light (sunrise), across it (midday), and out of it (sunset). Changing how fast Earth spins changes how long a day is.

## What this can't show
- **It is an illustration, not evidence.** The simulation shows what a spinning Earth *would* look like, because it was programmed to spin. The evidence that the real Earth spins is Foucault's pendulum and NASA's photos of the whole Earth from space. See "How do we know?" in the lesson.
- **The view from the ground.** You watch from space, so you don't see the Sun rise over a horizon. The Sky Lab *Manor Sunrise* module shows the backyard view in Manor at the same moment the terminator passes.
- **Why day length changes through the year.** That comes from Earth's tilt, which is Unit 3 (seasons).
- **True scale.** The Sun and Earth are much farther apart, compared to their sizes, than they look on screen. Unit 2 (Scale Ladder) covers that.
- **Gradual twilight.** The simulation's day–night line may look sharper or softer than the real sky. Real twilight comes from Earth's air scattering sunlight (lesson "Go deeper").
