# Universe Sandbox: Orientation (Unit 0)

**Goal:** Learn the five moves every later walkthrough assumes: open a simulation, pause and change speed, move the camera, select an object and open its properties, and find the in-app Guides and Help.
**Start from:** The Home menu, with the app freshly launched.
**Time:** about 15 minutes.

> **Draft status.** Every step below is **[unverified]**. It was written from secondhand sources (listed at the end), not from the app itself. The in-app Guides could not be read for this draft, because they exist only inside the app. Several keyboard shortcuts disagree between sources, so this draft teaches the mouse and on-screen buttons first and treats keys as "check live." We fix every step on Belu's screen during the live session, then stamp the file.

## Before you start

0. **Do:** Note the version number (shown on the title screen, or under Home > What's New). **You should see:** something like "Update 36.3" or later. Write it here: `________` [unverified] [S2][S7]

## Steps

### 1. Find the Home menu and open a simulation

1. **Do:** Click the **Home** button in the **upper-left corner** (three horizontal lines). **You should see:** a menu with items such as Open, Guides, Settings, Help, What's New, and Exit. [unverified] [S2]
2. **Do:** Click **Open** and choose a simulation that contains Earth and the Moon (for example, one named "Earth & Moon" or "Solar System"; exact names to confirm live). **You should see:** the simulation load with Earth in view. [unverified] [S2]

### 2. Pause and play

3. **Do:** Press **Spacebar**. **You should see:** motion stop, and the **Play/Pause** button in the **bottom bar** change its icon. All sources agree on this key. [unverified] [S1][S2][S3]
4. **Do:** Press **Spacebar** again, or click the **Play/Pause** button in the bottom bar. **You should see:** motion resume. [unverified] [S2]

> **Tip:** Pause before you try to click anything that moves. It makes selecting much easier.

### 3. Move the camera (mouse first)

5. **Do:** **Click and drag** with the left mouse button in empty space. **You should see:** the view swing around the object in the center. [unverified] [S1][S4]
6. **Do:** Roll the **mouse wheel**. **You should see:** the camera zoom toward or away from the center object. [unverified] [S1]
7. **Do (keys, check live):** Try the **arrow keys**. Older sources say Up/Down zoom and Left/Right rotate around the locked object. Sources disagree about **W/A/S/D**, so we will look them up under Home > Help (step 14) rather than guess. [unverified] [S3][S4]

### 4. Select an object and center the camera on it

8. **Do:** With time paused, **left-click Earth**. **You should see:** an outline appear around Earth (since Update 36.2 it follows the object's shape rather than drawing a circle), and a small info panel. [unverified] [S1][S6]
9. **Do:** **Double-click Earth** (some sources say "click the already-selected object again"). **You should see:** the camera center and lock on Earth, so dragging now orbits around Earth. [unverified] [S1][S4]
10. **Do (alternative):** Find Earth in the sim's **Search** list and click it. **You should see:** the camera move to Earth. [unverified] [S2]

### 5. Open the object's properties

11. **Do:** **Right-click Earth** (or click its small info panel). **You should see:** the **Object Properties** panel open, with a preview at the top and tabs below (one tab should be **Motion**). [unverified] [S1][S2][S4]
12. **Do:** Click through the tabs without changing anything. Find one value you recognize (such as mass or radius), then close the panel. **You should see:** the panel close; since Update 35 the app also moves and closes panels on its own. [unverified] [S2][S5]

> **Safety habit:** If you change a value by accident, use **Restart** (step 13) to put the simulation back.

### 6. Change the speed of time

13. **Do:** Click the **time display** in the **bottom bar** (the date and speed readout). **You should see:** a **Time** menu with **Restart**, **Reverse Time**, **Play/Pause**, **Step**, **Slower / Faster**, and **Target Simulation Speed**. [unverified] [S2]
    - Click **Faster** a few times. **You should see:** the speed readout rise and the Moon move visibly around Earth.
    - Click **Slower** until Earth's spin is easy to watch.
    - Click **Restart** to return to the starting moment.
    - Keyboard shortcuts for speed conflict between sources (`.` and `,` in one; `E`/`D` or Numpad `+`/`-` in the Legacy list). Check live in step 14. [S1][S3]

### 7. Find Guides and Help

14. **Do:** Open **Home > Help**. **You should see:** the **Input** tab of Settings, listing the current keyboard shortcuts. **Live task:** read off the keys for camera movement and for faster/slower, and we will write them into this file. [unverified] [S1][S2][S3]
15. **Do:** Open **Home > Guides**. **You should see:** a list of guided tutorials and lessons. **Live task:** read the titles aloud so we can list them here and pick any that fit later lessons. Then run the first, basic guide if there is one. [unverified] [S2][S8]

## What to look for

- Pausing changes nothing about the simulation; it just freezes time so you can look and click.
- Dragging and zooming move **you** (the camera), not the planets.
- Speeding up time makes slow motions (Earth's spin, the Moon's orbit) fast enough to see.

## What it means

Universe Sandbox is a **simulation**: a computer model that calculates how objects move under gravity and shows the result. You can look from any angle and run time at any speed, which you can never do with the real sky. That is why later lessons use it to *illustrate* ideas such as Earth's spin.

## What this can't show

- **A simulation is not evidence.** It shows what the physics rules predict; it does not prove that the real Earth behaves that way. Each lesson's "How do we know?" section carries the real evidence.
- **The view from the ground.** Universe Sandbox shows the solar system from space. What sunrise looks like from your own backyard is covered by the Sky Lab's **Manor Sunrise** module (`specs/`).
- **Real speed.** Sped-up time can make motions look jerky or skipped. Real Earth takes about 24 hours to spin once.
